# The value first, then the words

## 1. Find the value from the product itself

Read the product, not only its pitch: its roadmap and stages, open and closed tasks, the tool's own
journal and measurements, what it is used for every day. Answer five questions in a note:

1. **Idea.** What problem does it remove, in one sentence a stranger understands.
2. **Why needed.** Who suffers without it and how it shows in their day.
3. **Why it works.** The mechanism, in 2-4 points, each one a thing the product really does.
4. **Proof.** Numbers from real use with date, sample and method; a claim's condition is part of it.
5. **Against the alternatives.** Each kind of alternative and what it lacks that this product has.

Then where it grows (the roadmap), said as direction, not as promises.

Show the person the five answers in plain words and ask whether this is the value. Their correction
is the note's next version. Keep the note in the product's project (with XBrain: a note
`<Project>/Цінність і позиціювання <Product>`), and every page and post is written from it.

## 2. Words on a design

- Plain and factual. The person's image ("a place where all roads meet") explains the essence to
  you; on the page say what the product does.
- The person's language first; every language of the product, synchronized in meaning, not word for word.
- No long dash in the person's languages where their rules forbid it.
- A headline says the benefit; the line under it says how. A chapter is one idea: a big title and two
  or three lines.
- Numbers: few and strong on a marketing page (about three), each with a source and a "how measured"
  link; tables and charts belong on a separate page.
- No promise the product does not keep today. What is coming is named as coming.
- Calls to action name the action ("Get early access"), and the page has one primary action.
- Avoid template phrases ("revolutionary", "seamless", "unlock"), invented testimonials and fake urgency.
